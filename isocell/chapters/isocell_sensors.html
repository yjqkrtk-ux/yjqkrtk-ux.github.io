16-Channel Periodic Phase Decomposition & Lossless In-Sensor Zoom

In a $4\times 4$ Hexadeca mosaic, each pixel $(y, x)$ within a $4\times 4$ block has a distinct spatial offset $(\Delta y, \Delta x) \in \{0,1,2,3\}^2$ relative to the macro color block boundary. Applying a standard translation-invariant $3\times 3$ convolution directly on the 2D mosaic mixes interior pixels with boundary pixels. Instead, periodic PixelUnshuffle decomposes the $(1, H, W)$ mosaic into a 16-channel phase-homogeneous tensor:

$$\mathcal{U}_{4\times 4} : \mathbb{R}^{1 \times H \times W} \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{R}^{16 \times \frac{H}{4} \times \frac{W}{4}}, \qquad \mathbf{X}_{c}(u, v) = I_{\text{RAW}}\!\left(4u + \lfloor c/4 \rfloor,\; 4v + (c \bmod 4)\right)$$

Every channel $c \in \{0,\dots,15\}$ now represents a uniform spatial sub-phase at $\frac{1}{16}$ spatial area, enabling compact residual NPU blocks to predict full-resolution Bayer/RGB phases before PixelShuffle ($\mathcal{U}_{4\times 4}^{-1}$) reassembles the 200MP grid. For 2× (50MP) and 4× (12.5MP) In-Sensor Zoom—especially critical on ISOCELL HP9 200MP Telephoto (vivo X200 Pro ZEISS) and ISOCELL HP2 (Galaxy S23 Ultra)—only the central ROI of the sensor is read out in $1\times 1$ unbinned mode and remosaiced, delivering lossless optical-equivalent zoom up to 4×–10×.
