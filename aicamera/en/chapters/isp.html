CHAPTER 03 · SOFTWARE ISP & HALIDE/NPU OPTIMIZATION (hdrnet/)

Software ISP — From C99 Reference to Fused Halide & Edge AI

The raw uint16 Bayer RGGB array emitted by a sensor is, by itself, merely a dark, green-tinted mosaic grid. In this chapter, we ① dissect the exact mathematical specification of our 10-stage C99 reference OpenISP pipeline and 3A closed-loop controller, and ② use interactive simulators to explore how a pipeline that originally consumed 1,257.8 MB/frame of round-trip DRAM traffic is compressed down to 91.3 MB/frame (13.8× reduction) through Verified Lifting, Halide kernel fusion, and DMA-BUF Zero-Copy.

3.1 Why Linear Correction Comes First — 10-Stage Pipeline Domain Structure

The ordering of ISP stages is not arbitrary; it is strictly dictated by the physical linearity of photoelectric conversion. Subtracting black level (BLC) and multiplying lens shading (LSC) and white balance (AWB) gains must happen in the linear Bayer RAW domain (Stages 1–5)—where sample values are directly proportional to photon counts—so radiometric ratios remain undistorted. Only after demosaicing (Stage 6) and the color correction matrix (Stage 7) complete in linear RGB are the nonlinear gamma curve (Stage 8), YUV color space conversion (Stage 9), and luma edge enhancement (Stage 10) applied.

3.3 Stage-by-Stage Mathematical Formulas & C99 Implementation Details

The 10 cards below give the exact mathematical definitions and default register values corresponding 1:1 to the C functions implemented in openisp_procedural.c.

1

Defective Pixel Correction (DPC)

Default off · DRAM 4 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:42
\[ \hat p=\begin{cases}\tfrac12\big(n_{(4)}+n_{(5)}\big) & p \gt n_{(8)}+T\ \ \lor\ \ p \lt n_{(1)}-T\\ p & \text{otherwise}\end{cases} \]

Sorts the 8 same-color neighbors (±2 px) into \(n_{(1)}\le\dots\le n_{(8)}\). If pixel \(p\) exceeds the neighbor range by more than \(T\)=dpc_threshold(30.0), it is replaced by the average of the two median neighbors. Because \(T\) is expressed in uint16 storage units (10-bit×64), it corresponds to ~0.47 DN in 10-bit units.

2

Black Level Compensation (BLC)

Always On · DRAM 6 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:98
\[ x=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(\frac{I-64B}{64W-64B}\right) \]

\(B\)=black_level(64.0), \(W\)=white_level(1023.0). When is_16bit_input is set, 10-bit samples are treated as MSB-aligned in 16-bit (scaled by ×64) and normalized into linear [0,1] floating-point values.

3

Lens Shading Correction (LSC)

Default off · DRAM 8 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:124
\[ x'=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\big(x\,(1+a_1 r^2+a_2 r^4)\big),\qquad r^2=(u-c_x)^2+(v-c_y)^2 \]

Squared radius in pixels \(r^2\) around the optical center \(c=((W-1)/2,(H-1)/2)\). \(a_1\)=1.5e-7, \(a_2\)=2.0e-14. Multiplies a radially symmetric gain equally across color channels.

4

Anti-Noise Filter (Bayer Bilateral ANF)

Default off · DRAM 8 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:160
\[ x'(p)=\frac{\sum_{q} w_{pq}\,x(q)}{\sum_{q} w_{pq}},\qquad w_{pq}=\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\lVert d_{pq}\rVert^2}{2\sigma_s^2}-\frac{(x(q)-x(p))^2}{2\sigma_r^2}\Big) \]

Suppresses noise while preserving edges across a same-color 3×3 lattice (±2 px physical spacing). \(d\) is measured in lattice steps (−1, 0, 1). \(\sigma_s\)=1.0, \(\sigma_r\)=0.05.

5

White Balance & Digital Gain (AWB)

Always On · DRAM 8 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:214
\[ x'=x\cdot g_c\cdot g_d,\qquad g_c\in\{g_R,\ g_G,\ g_B\} \]

\(g_c\) is selected according to the RGGB 2×2 phase. Default \(g_R\)=2.20, \(g_G\)=1.00, \(g_B\)=1.80, \(g_d\)=1.00. No clipping is applied at this stage; when the 3A loop is active, gains are updated every frame from 3A outputs.

6

Demosaic (Branchless Bilinear, Fixed RGGB)

Always On · DRAM 16 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:250
\[ \begin{aligned} G_{@R}&=\tfrac14\big(G_N+G_S+G_W+G_E\big)\\ B_{@R}&=\tfrac14\big(B_{NW}+B_{NE}+B_{SW}+B_{SE}\big)\\ R_{@G_r}&=\tfrac12\big(R_W+R_E\big) \end{aligned} \]

Branchless bilinear interpolation operating on 2×2 macropixels. Produces interleaved RGB float32 clipped to [0,1]. (Early design notes mentioning Malvar-He-Cutler differ from the actual C implementation.)

7

Color Correction Matrix (CCM)

Always On · DRAM 24 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:343
\[ \begin{bmatrix}R'\\G'\\B'\end{bmatrix}=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\!\left(M\begin{bmatrix}R\\G\\B\end{bmatrix}\right) \]\[ M_{\mathrm{D65}}=\begin{bmatrix}1.6605 & -0.5876 & -0.0728\\-0.1245 & 1.3038 & -0.1793\\-0.0181 & -0.5055 & 1.5236\end{bmatrix} \]

Rotates and aligns sensor spectral sensitivities into the standard sRGB gamut. When 3A is active, \(M\) is linearly interpolated across 3000K–7500K presets based on estimated CCT. The saturation field is unused in the C path.

8

Gamma Tone Mapping (Pure Power Law LUT)

Always On · DRAM 24 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:376
\[ \begin{aligned} y&=\mathrm{LUT}\big[\operatorname{round}(1023\,x)\big]\\ \mathrm{LUT}[i]&=\Big(\frac{i}{1023}\Big)^{1/\gamma} \end{aligned} \]

1024-entry lookup table with \(\gamma\)=gamma(2.2). Implements a pure power law rather than the piecewise sRGB OETF with a linear toe.

9

Color Space Conversion (BT.601 Limited YUV420)

Always On · DRAM 13.5 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:409
\[ \begin{aligned} Y&=16+65.481\,R'+128.553\,G'+24.966\,B'\\ C_b&=128-37.797\,\bar R'-74.203\,\bar G'+112\,\bar B'\\ C_r&=128+112\,\bar R'-93.786\,\bar G'-18.214\,\bar B' \end{aligned} \]

\(\bar R',\bar G',\bar B'\) denote 2×2 block averages (4:2:0 chroma subsampling). Follows studio swing (Y 16–235), matching OpenCV COLOR_YUV2BGR_I420 exactly. Output is planar YUV420 uint8.

10

Edge Enhancement (EEH on Y Luma, Integer Fixed-Point)

Default off · DRAM 2 B/px · openisp_procedural.c:468
\[ \begin{aligned} h&=\operatorname{clip}_{[-c,\,c]}\!\Big(Y-\Big\lfloor\tfrac19\textstyle\sum_{3\times3}Y\Big\rfloor\Big)\\ Y'&=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,255]}\!\big(Y+((h\cdot g_{fp})\gg 8)\big) \end{aligned} \]

\(g_{fp}=\operatorname{round}(256k)\), \(c=\operatorname{round}(255\cdot\text{clip})\); default \(k\)=eeh_gain(0.8), clip=eeh_clip(0.15). Operates directly on the uint8 luma (Y) plane using fixed-point integer arithmetic.

3.4 3A Closed-Loop Auto Control — AWB · CCT-Interpolated CCM · AE

When a camera encounters varying illuminants (incandescent 3000K to overcast 7500K) and brightness shifts, the feedback controller that stabilizes color and exposure is the 3A loop (openisp_3a.c). Every frame, our implementation samples RGGB quads at stats_stride (8 px) intervals in the linear domain right after BLC to compute channel means \((\bar{R}, \bar{G}, \bar{B})\) and Rec.709 luma \(L = 0.2126\bar{R} + 0.7152\bar{G} + 0.0722\bar{B}\) (openisp_3a.c:35).

1) Gray-World AWB & Adaptive EMA

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat g_R &= \operatorname{clip}_{[0.5,\,4]}\!\Big(\frac{\bar G}{\bar R}\Big),\qquad \hat g_B = \operatorname{clip}_{[0.5,\,4]}\!\Big(\frac{\bar G}{\bar B}\Big)\\ g_t &= \alpha\,\hat g + (1-\alpha)\,g_{t-1} \end{aligned} \]

Under large scene shifts (\(|\hat{g} - g_{t-1}| \gt 0.3\)), the filter converges rapidly with \(\alpha = 0.40\); during steady state, it tracks smoothly using awb_speed (0.15). If mean luma \(L\) drops below dark_luma_threshold (0.02), AWB gains are frozen to prevent noise amplification.

2) Estimated CCT-Driven Linear CCM Interpolation

\[ T_{cc} = \operatorname{clip}_{[2400,\,8500]}\!\big(5500\cdot g_R / g_B\big) \]

Using the estimated correlated color temperature \(T_{cc}\), the controller piecewise-linearly interpolates across four standard CCM presets at 3000K, 4000K, 6500K (D65), and 7500K (openisp_3a.c:327).

3) Digital-Gain AE & Adaptive EMA

\[ \begin{aligned} \hat d &= \operatorname{clip}_{[0.25,\,8]}\!\Big(\frac{0.18\cdot 2^{\mathrm{EV}}}{\max(10^{-3},\,L)}\Big)\\ d_t &= \beta\,\hat d + (1-\beta)\,d_{t-1} \end{aligned} \]

Uses \(\beta = 0.45\) under large exposure errors (\(|\hat{d} - d_{t-1}| \gt 0.4\)) and ae_speed (0.20) otherwise. (Note: the C 3A loop updates digital gain only and does not control sensor shutter integration time, analog gain, or autofocus.)

3.5 4-Tier Execution Engines, Halide Kernel Fusion & DMA-BUF Zero-Copy

When processing a 12.33 MP (4056×3040) high-resolution frame by writing and reading full-frame buffers to and from DRAM at every stage like the C99 reference (Tier 1), each frame incurs roughly 102 B/px (1,257.8 MB) of DRAM traffic, thrashing the CPU cache at every stage boundary. To overcome this, we progressively optimized the exact same mathematical pipeline across four execution tiers (Tiers 1–4):

3.6 Verified Lifting — Automated C99-to-Halide Translation & Numerical Verification

Manually rewriting C loops into the Halide DSL is prone to index offset and boundary condition bugs. The hdrnet/verified_lifting/ module parses the C99 reference source, classifies operator patterns, automatically emits Halide code and schedules, and verifies numerical equivalence:

4-Stage Automated Lifting Pipeline

  1. C AST Parsing: Regex and syntax analyzers extract C stage function signatures and nested loop bounds (ast_parser.py).
  2. Operator Pattern Classification: Normalizes loop bodies into Pointwise, Stencil (neighbor access), Reduction, and LUT intermediate representations (IR) (patterns.py).
  3. Halide Code & Schedule Generation: Emits Halide::Func/Var C++ code (halide_emitter.py) and binds target hardware schedules (CPU NEON / GPU Vulkan) (schedule_library.py).
  4. Numerical Parity Verifier: Measures the error between C reference outputs and Halide outputs across random and test-pattern inputs, enforcing PSNR ≥ 40.0 dB and MAE ≤ 10⁻³ (verifier.py:42).
Verification Scope Clarification (Numerical Parity vs Formal Proof)

In the current repository, verifier.py (verifier.py:42) performs automated numerical parity verification over test input tensors. No Z3 SMT solver or SyGuS symbolic formal proof engine is included in the codebase; therefore, "Verified Lifting" in project documents denotes our pattern-based automated transpilation + numerical regression verification pipeline.

Key Takeaways · Chapter 03

  • Linear-Domain-First Principle: BLC, LSC, ANF, and AWB (Stages 2–5) along with Demosaic and CCM (Stages 6–7) must operate in the photon-linear domain to prevent distortion of color ratios and radial shading correction, placing nonlinear Gamma (Stage 8) and YUV conversion (Stage 9) at the end.
  • Adaptive 3A Feedback Loop: Gray-World AWB and digital-gain AE suppress flicker during steady state using low EMA factors (\(\alpha=0.15, \beta=0.20\)), and immediately switch to fast factors (\(0.40 / 0.45\)) upon detecting large illumination steps (\(|\Delta| \gt 0.3 / 0.4\)).
  • Halide Kernel Fusion & DMA-BUF: Fusing the 12MP pipeline—which previously consumed 1,257.8 MB of stage-by-stage DRAM round-trips—into a single tiled loop (Tier 3) slashes DRAM traffic to 91.3 MB (13.8× reduction) and accelerates CPU execution from 493.85 ms → 121.48 ms (4.07× speedup).
  • Dual-NPU Async Edge AI: Running IMX500 (in-sensor detection) and Hailo-8 (Bilateral Grid / segmentation) on asynchronous threads decoupled from the ISP loop, combined with ZAP EMA viewport tracking, delivers drop-free intelligent camera UX.
