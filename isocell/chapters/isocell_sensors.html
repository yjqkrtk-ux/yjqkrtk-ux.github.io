What Is Remosaic? (Core Principle & Multi-Mode Readout)

To fit 200 million pixels ($16{,}320 \times 12{,}288$) into a mobile camera module, individual pixel pitch is scaled down to the sub-micron regime (0.60µm on HP2, 0.56µm on HP3/HP9, and 0.50µm on HP5). Instead of using a conventional $2\times 2$ Bayer Color Filter Array (CFA), 200MP ISOCELL sensors adopt a Non-Bayer Multi-Cell CFA—specifically Tetra²pixel ($4\times 4$ Hexadeca / Q-cell, 16-cell), where $4\times 4 = 16$ neighboring photodiodes share the same color filter (R, G, or B).

  • Why Non-Bayer CFA Is Used (Pixel Binning in Low Light): In dark or everyday environments, the sensor merges (bins) $4\times 4 = 16$ same-color pixels into a single large super-pixel (12.5MP output) or $2\times 2 = 4$ pixels (50MP output). Because binned pixels already form a regular Bayer pattern, the frame can be sent directly to the standard Camera ISP with high sensitivity.
  • Why Remosaic Is Required (Full 200MP & 2×/4× In-Sensor Zoom): Standard mobile Camera ISPs can only process a conventional $2\times 2$ Bayer (RGGB) pattern. When capturing a full 200MP ultra-high-resolution photo or cropping the center of the sensor for lossless $2\times$ / $4\times$ In-Sensor Zoom, the raw sensor readout is still arranged in a $4\times 4$ non-Bayer mosaic. Remosaic is the core algorithm that reconstructs a sharp, artifact-free $2\times 2$ Bayer CFA pattern from the non-Bayer $4\times 4$ RAW data before (or within) the Camera ISP pipeline.
