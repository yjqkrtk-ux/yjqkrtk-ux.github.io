Overcoming the 200MP Serial Capture Bottleneck (HetSoC Parallel Architecture)

A single 200MP 10-bit RAW frame (16,320 × 12,288) occupies ~250 MB in unpacked 16-bit memory, and a 3-channel 200MP RGB/YUV frame occupies ~600 MB. In a conventional serial pipeline, the system must finish full-frame DL Remosaic and flush the reconstructed 200MP Bayer frame back to LPDDR DRAM before the ISP can begin denoising, tone mapping, and color conversion—resulting in multiple full-frame DRAM round-trips and ~3.0 seconds of capture latency.

$$T_{\text{serial}} = T_{\text{RAW-DMA}} + T_{\text{DL-Remosaic}} + T_{\text{DRAM-Flush}} + T_{\text{SW/HW-ISP}} \;\approx\; 3.0\text{ s}$$ $$T_{\text{E2E-Parallel}} = \max_{k \in \{\text{NPU},\,\text{GPU},\,\text{ISP}\}}\!\left(T_{\text{tile}, k}\right) \cdot N_{\text{tiles}} + T_{\text{pipe-fill}} \;\approx\; 1.0\text{ s}\quad (\text{Up to } 3\times \text{ faster})$$

As publicly announced by Samsung Semiconductor Global (“Samsung Electronics Introduces ISOCELL Zoom Anyplace & E2E AI Remosaic Solutions for the 200-Megapixel Image Sensor”), E2E AI Remosaic partitions the 200MP frame into overlapping spatial tiles and streams them concurrently across heterogeneous mobile SoC engines—CPU, GPU, NPU, and ISP (Qualcomm Snapdragon & MediaTek Dimensity)—overlapping neural remosaicing with ISP tone/color processing.

In single-frame 200MP capture, sub-micron shot noise at $0.50\text{–}0.56\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ and optical diffraction limit high-frequency texture recovery under indoor or high-zoom conditions. However, running single-frame DL Remosaic on $K$ burst frames separately would multiply NPU latency by $K\times$.

E2E AI Remosaic Gen 4 (Jan 2026 – Present, deployed on HONOR Magic 9 & Magic 9 Pro Max) introduces DL-Based Multi-Frame Remosaic directly in the non-Bayer phase domain. Given $K$ burst RAW frames $\left\{I_{\text{RAW}}^{(k)}\right\}_{k=1}^K$ with sub-pixel hand-tremor offsets $\mathbf{V}_k \in \mathbb{R}^{2 \times H/4 \times W/4}$, the network aligns phase tensors in the 16-channel PixelUnshuffle space and jointly executes demosaicing, super-resolution detail fusion, and temporal noise reduction in a single NPU pass:

$$\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{\text{YUV}} = \mathcal{P}_{\text{ISP}}\!\left(\mathcal{U}_{4\times 4}^{-1}\!\left(\mathcal{F}_{\theta}^{\text{MF-NPU}}\!\left(\mathcal{U}_{4\times 4}\!\left(I_{\text{RAW}}^{(1)}\right),\; \left\{\mathcal{W}\!\left(\mathcal{U}_{4\times 4}\!\left(I_{\text{RAW}}^{(k)}\right),\; \mathbf{V}_k\right)\right\}_{k=2}^K\right)\right)\right)$$
