CHAPTER 02 · LATENT SIGNAL PROCESSING (lsp/)

Latent Signal Processing — Asymmetric Sensor–Host Split Encoder

Conventional cameras finish rendering pixels on the sensor, transmit the full frame to the host, and then run a heavy neural encoder on the host to extract a latent representation. Latent Signal Processing (LSP) eliminates this redundancy. By asymmetrically factoring the encoder across the sensor–SoC boundary, the sensor-die NPU (Stage A) compresses bus bandwidth by up to 48×, while the host NPU (Stage B) translates it into the standard Stable Diffusion VAE latent space (kl-f8, \(\alpha=0.18215\))—unifying a 30 FPS live viewfinder and Zero-Shutter-Lag (ZSL) generative capture in a single pipeline.

2.1 Why Split the Encoder — Bus Bottleneck & Regime Shift

When running neural ISPs or generative models on an edge camera, the first wall you hit is not compute (TOPS) but inter-chip serial interface and host DMA extraction latency. Whereas the uncompressed IDENTITY baseline (\(s_A=1\)) transmits 3,072 KB per frame, SA8 (\(s_A=8\)) with in-sensor spatial compression shrinks the payload by exactly 48× to 64 KB.

Bandwidth-Limited Regime (\(s_A = 1, 2\))

In IDENTITY (3,072 KB) and SA2 (1,024 KB), the time spent by the host extracting the metadata tensor (bus_ms = 52.91 ms and 17.63 ms) dominates the entire pipeline. Even with a 26 TOPS Hailo-8 accelerator attached to the host, frame rate remains locked at 14.9–15.2 FPS.

Compute-Balanced Regime (\(s_A = 4, 8\))

Compressing to SA4 (256 KB, 6.23 ms) and SA8 (64 KB, 2.90 ms) makes bus_ms shorter than the host decoder (taesd_hailo_ms = 16.44 ms). The bottleneck shifts from the bus to NPU compute, and SA8 saturates the camera's physical ceiling (30.0 FPS) with a pipelined compute throughput of 43.7 FPS.

2.3 Stage A Compressor & Stage B Translator — Mathematical & QAT Specification

The LSP encoder approximates the mapping of an input image \(x \in \mathbb{R}^{512\times 512\times 3}\) as the composition of a sensor-side compressor \(\mathcal{E}_A\) and a host-side translator \(\mathcal{T}_B\). The teacher signal is the latent tensor \(z\) of the pre-trained Stable Diffusion VAE encoder (\(\alpha = 0.18215\)):

$$r = \mathcal{E}_A(x) \in \mathbb{R}^{\frac{512}{s_A}\times\frac{512}{s_A}\times 4}, \qquad \hat{z} = \mathcal{T}_B(r) \in \mathbb{R}^{64\times 64\times 4}, \qquad z = 0.18215\cdot \mathrm{VAE}_{\mathrm{enc}}(x)$$ $$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{train}} = \lVert \hat{z} - z \rVert_1$$
Although the training script (train_asym_ae.py) also includes a perceptual loss (LPIPS) routine, it executes inside a z.detach() and torch.no_grad() block, so backpropagation gradients are computed purely from the latent-space L1 loss.

Hailo-8 Compatible Power-of-2 Quantization-Aware Training (QAT)

To minimize multiplier hardware overhead, the Hailo-8 NPU dataflow path restricts inter-layer scale factors to powers of two (bit shifts). Under standard floating-point training followed by post-training quantization (PTQ), even a slight outlier spike in \(\max|x|\) forces the scale \(s\) to jump by \(2\times\) to the next power of two, sacrificing 1 full bit of effective quantization precision across the bulk of the distribution. To prevent this, Stage B (encoder.py:19) simulates power-of-2 scale quantization in the forward pass and passes gradients via the Straight-Through Estimator (STE) in the backward pass:

$$s = 2^{\left\lceil \log_2\left(\max|x| / 127\right)\right\rceil}, \qquad x_q = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\operatorname{round}(x/s),\,-128,\,127\big)\cdot s$$

2.6 Zero-Shutter-Lag (ZSL) Generative Capture & ControlNet Conditional Diffusion

Performing high-quality generative photography on a conventional camera requires pressing the shutter and then waiting for ① sensor re-exposure, ② multi-MB RAW re-transmission, and ③ host VAE encoder re-execution. LSP's Zero-Shutter-Lag (ZSL) capture simply freezes a snapshot of the \(\hat{z}\) tensor and preview image that are already resident in host memory every frame during live viewfinder streaming.

ZSL 6-Stage Execution Sequence (generative_capture_service.py)

  1. Edge Snapshot: When the user triggers capture, the current frame's 03_hailo_stageb_latent_z.npy (\(\hat{z}\)) and 04_hailo_taesd_preview.png are immediately flushed to disk (zero re-exposure, zero re-transfer).
  2. Async Watcher: The workstation's generative_capture_service.py --watch daemon detects the new capture folder and pulls it via scp.
  3. Init Image Reconstruction: \(\hat{z} / 0.18215\) is decoded via pipe.vae.decode() and set as the initial image of the img2img pipeline.
  4. Structural Condition Extraction: Extracts a cv2.Canny(gray, 70, 160) edge map from the preview image as the ControlNet conditioning input.
  5. Fast Diffusion Synthesis: Runs stable-diffusion-v1-5 + sd-controlnet-canny with DPM-Solver++ in 4 steps (strength 0.70, guidance 7.5).
  6. 4-Panel Gallery Composite: Composites Original · Preview · Canny · Generated Result, saves to disk, and pushes back to the RPi web UI.
Code-vs-Paper Cross-Check Note — How the Latent Tensor Is Consumed

Conceptually, \(\hat{z}\) is directly compatible with the diffusion model's latent space; in the actual implementation (generative_capture_service.py), because we use Diffusers' standard StableDiffusionControlNetImg2ImgPipeline, \(\hat{z}\) is first decoded once by the VAE decoder into an image and then re-encoded inside the pipeline. Thus "zero sensor re-exposure · zero MIPI re-transfer · zero edge encoder re-execution" holds strictly on the camera/edge side, while a single decode/re-encode pass occurs on the workstation.

Key Takeaways · Chapter 02

  1. Breaking the Bus Bottleneck & Regime Shift: SA8 (\(64\times 64\times 4\) FP32, 64 KB) cuts MIPI metadata extraction time by 18.2× from 52.91 ms to 2.90 ms, shifting the system bottleneck from the bus to NPU compute and hitting the 30.0 FPS camera ceiling.
  2. Extreme Asymmetric Split: Only a 5,924-parameter (9.59K graph-exported) pure strided convolution stack (Stage A) tailored to SRAM and operator constraints runs on the IMX500 sensor NPU, while nonlinear latent space alignment is handled by the 595,524-parameter ResNet translator (Stage B) on the Hailo-8 host NPU.
  3. Power-of-2 QAT: Simulating Hailo hardware bit-shift scales (\(s = 2^{\lceil\log_2(\max|x|/127)\rceil}\)) via STE during training prevents precision loss from scale jumps during quantization.
  4. Universal Latent Hub & ZSL Capture: Because Stage B output is aligned with the shared SD 1.5 / SD 2.1 kl-f8 VAE latent space (\(\alpha=0.18215\)), the edge live preview (TAESD_ft) and workstation generative capture (SD 1.5 + ControlNet Canny) share the exact same latent tensor without extra sensor exposure or re-transmission.
