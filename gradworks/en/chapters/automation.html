Low-Level Input Driver Hooking & 13-State Priority Vision State Machine

In DirectX-based client environments, standard operating-system virtual keyboard events (keybd_event) are blocked by the DirectInput polling loop. To overcome this limitation, CMaple (C#) and PyMaple (pydetect.py:4) layer a hardware scancode (DIK Scancode) injection driver with an OpenCV + Faster R-CNN multi-scale vision feedback loop.

Absorbing Markov Chain (StarForce) & Non-Markovian Chance Time State Augmentation

StarForce.m:1 is a stochastic model simulating equipment enhancement from 0★ to 22★ (target absorbing state). Defining the state solely by the current star level $L \in \{0, \dots, 21\}$ violates the first-order Markov property because of the Chance Time (if(fail == 2)) rule implemented in StarForce.m:24:

  • When enhancement failure causes the star level to drop twice consecutively (fail == 2), the conditional success probability on the very next attempt becomes $100\%$ ($1.0$).
  • In the $12 \sim 21$★ range, hitting the destruction probability $p_{\text{dest}}(L)$ destroys ("booms") the item and immediately resets it to $12$★ (level = 12; fail = 0;, StarForce.m:42).
  • Succeeding at the StarCatch mini-game (StarForce.m:10) multiplies the base success rate by $1 + \text{starcatch} = 1.05$: $$p_{\text{succ}}'(L) = \min\!\left(1.0,\; p_{\text{succ}}(L) \cdot (1 + 0.05)\right)$$

To transform the process into a rigorous first-order Absorbing Markov Chain, we define an augmented state space $s = (L, f) \in \{0,\dots,21\} \times \{0, 1, 2\}$ (where $f$ counts consecutive level drops). From the transient submatrix $\mathbf{Q}$ of the transition matrix $\mathbf{P}$, both the Fundamental Matrix $\mathbf{N}$ and the expected total cost $\mathbb{E}[C]$ can be solved analytically:

$$\mathbf{N} = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q})^{-1}, \qquad \mathbb{E}\!\left[\text{Total Cost from } s_0 \to 22\text{-star}\right] = \sum_{s' \in \mathcal{T}} N_{s_0, s'} \, c(s')$$

Constrained Combinatorial Optimization (Lophago) & Casio FX-9860G Bare-Metal Engine

Beyond stochastic modeling, Themes 3 and 4 address discrete combinatorial search and ultra-low-power embedded optimization:

  • Lophago Engraving Combination Solver (main.py:119): Across ITEM_NUM = 5 equipment slots (1 necklace, 2 earrings, 2 rings), the solver combines candidate Relic allocations (ENGRAVING_COMB_RELIC: $[3,3], [4,3], [5,3]$, etc.) and Ancient allocations (ENGRAVING_COMB_LEGENDARY: including $[6,3]$) with ability stone and engraving book points, using pruned backtracking to enumerate all valid combinations satisfying a target engraving vector (e.g., $33333$ or $333331$).
  • Casio FX-9860G Native Game Engines (Casio RPG/README.md, Casio RTS/README.md): Built in bare-metal C for a graphing calculator equipped only with a Renesas SuperH-3 CPU and a $128 \times 64$ 1-bit monochrome LCD ($1{,}024\text{ Bytes}$ framebuffer), implementing cross-byte bitwise shift sprite blitting (Bit-Blit), tilemap scrolling, and multi-entity real-time strategy (RTS) unit loops without external engines.

WINNER II 3D MIMO Geometry-Based Stochastic Channel & 60 GHz Millimeter-Wave Backhaul

WinnerIIChannel in Theme 4 (Main.m, Step1.m–Step11.m) is an 11-step Geometry-Based Stochastic Channel Model (GBSM) designed for 5G/B5G Massive MIMO and 60 GHz unlicensed small-cell wireless backhaul links. Between $S$ transmit antenna elements and $U$ receive antenna elements, the 3D channel impulse response across cluster $n \in \{1,\dots,N\}$ and subpath $m \in \{1,\dots,M\}$ is synthesized as:

$$H_{u, s, n}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{P_n}{M}} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \mathbf{F}_{\text{rx}, u}\!\left(\Omega_{n,m}^{\text{AOA}}\right)^T \mathbf{\Gamma}_{n,m} \, \mathbf{F}_{\text{tx}, s}\!\left(\Psi_{n,m}^{\text{AOD}}\right) e^{j k_0 \mathbf{r}_{\text{rx}, u}^T \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{n,m}} e^{j k_0 \mathbf{r}_{\text{tx}, s}^T \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{n,m}} e^{j 2\pi \nu_{n,m} t}$$
where $\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n,m} = \begin{bmatrix} e^{j\Phi_{n,m}^{vv}} & \sqrt{\kappa_{n,m}^{-1}}\,e^{j\Phi_{n,m}^{vh}} \\ \sqrt{\kappa_{n,m}^{-1}}\,e^{j\Phi_{n,m}^{hv}} & e^{j\Phi_{n,m}^{hh}} \end{bmatrix}$ is the cross-polarization coupling matrix, $\kappa_{n,m}$ is the Cross-Polarization Ratio (XPR), and $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{n,m}, \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{n,m} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are the 3D spherical unit direction vectors at the receiver and transmitter.
