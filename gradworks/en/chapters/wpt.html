To deliver 1 kW-class wireless power safely and efficiently to mobile electronics in free space, a transmitter must track the 3D position $(X, Y, Z)$ of the receiver coil with millimeter precision while immediately cutting off output power whenever a human or pet enters the strong electromagnetic field zone. Mathematical Derivation of Tsai Camera Calibration & Binocular 3D Skew-Ray Triangulation

Rather than relying on black-box external libraries, TsaiCalibration.py:4 directly implements a 4-stage pipeline that estimates the camera projection matrix from correspondences between 3D world coordinates $\mathbf{X}_i = [X_i, Y_i, Z_i]^T$ and 2D pixel coordinates $\mathbf{u}_i = [u_i, v_i]^T$, and then reconstructs 3D target positions in real time.

Step 1 · 11-DoF Linear Least-Squares Projection Matrix Estimation (Find_K_Matrix)

In homogeneous coordinates, the $3 \times 4$ pinhole camera projection matrix $K$ (scale-normalized by setting $K_{3,4} = 1$) satisfies:

$$s \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} k_{11} & k_{12} & k_{13} & k_{14} \\ k_{21} & k_{22} & k_{23} & k_{24} \\ k_{31} & k_{32} & k_{33} & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ Z \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Eliminating the scale factor $s = k_{31}X + k_{32}Y + k_{33}Z + 1$ yields two linear equations per 3D–2D point correspondence. Stacking $N$ calibration points forms a $2N \times 11$ design matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and a $2N \times 1$ observation vector $\mathbf{b}$ (TsaiCalibration.py:4). The least-squares solution via the normal equations is:

$$\mathbf{k}^* = \left(\mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{A}\right)^{-1} \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{R}^{11}$$

Step 2 · 3D Ray Back-Projection (Line_gen) & Closest-Midpoint Triangulation (Est_Point)

Given a pixel $(u, v)$ and calibrated projection matrix $K$, the first and second rows of the projection equation define two planes $\Pi_u$ and $\Pi_v$ in 3D space. Line_gen(K, u, v) in TsaiCalibration.py:41 takes the cross product of the two plane normal vectors $\mathbf{n}_u, \mathbf{n}_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ to obtain the 3D ray direction vector $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{n}_u \times \mathbf{n}_v$ and anchor point $\mathbf{p}_0$:

$$\mathbf{L}_1(t_1) = \mathbf{p}_1 + t_1 \mathbf{v}_1, \qquad \mathbf{L}_2(t_2) = \mathbf{p}_2 + t_2 \mathbf{v}_2$$

In physical deployments, lens distortion and pixel quantization noise prevent the two camera rays $\mathbf{L}_1(t_1)$ and $\mathbf{L}_2(t_2)$ from intersecting at a single point, making them skew lines in 3D space. Est_Point(Line1, Line2) in TsaiCalibration.py:58 enforces the orthogonality condition minimizing the squared Euclidean distance $\|\mathbf{L}_1(t_1) - \mathbf{L}_2(t_2)\|_2^2$ ($(\mathbf{L}_1 - \mathbf{L}_2) \perp \mathbf{v}_1$, $(\mathbf{L}_1 - \mathbf{L}_2) \perp \mathbf{v}_2$), solves the resulting $2 \times 2$ linear system for $(t_1^*, t_2^*)$, and determines the 3D receiver coil position $\mathbf{P}^*$ as the midpoint of the shortest connecting segment (TsaiCalibration.py:72):

$$\begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_1 & -\mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 \\ \mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 & -\mathbf{v}_2 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} t_1^* \\ t_2^* \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -(\mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_2) \cdot \mathbf{v}_1 \\ -(\mathbf{p}_1 - \mathbf{p}_2) \cdot \mathbf{v}_2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{P}^* = \frac{\mathbf{L}_1(t_1^*) + \mathbf{L}_2(t_2^*)}{2}$$

OLEV 85 kHz Dynamic Wireless Charging Telematics & UTM Trajectory Prediction

Keeping road-embedded segmented power rails (85 kHz, 100 kW class) continuously energized would cause severe standby power loss and unnecessary EMF exposure; therefore, each inverter segment must be pre-heated and switched on (Pre-Turn-On) just before the OLEV electric bus enters the segment. Telematics_and_GPS in Theme 1 solves this using 4 Android vehicle terminals (EVGPS, GPSlog, GPStracker, InverterGPS) and a MATLAB trajectory analysis engine (get_lane_distance.m:1, get_predicted_time.m:1).

Point-to-Line Orthogonal Lane Deviation & Interquartile 25% Trimmed Mean (get_lane_distance.m)

After projecting WGS84 latitude/longitude coordinates into metric 2D Cartesian coordinates $\mathbf{p}_i = (x_i, y_i)$ via deg2utm.m, the vehicle's orthogonal lateral deviation from the road centerline segment $(\mathbf{q}_1, \mathbf{q}_2)$ is computed via the 2D cross product (get_lane_distance.m:10):

$$d_{\perp, i} = \frac{\left|(x_{q_2} - x_{q_1})(y_{q_1} - y_i) - (x_{q_1} - x_i)(y_{q_2} - y_{q_1})\right|}{\sqrt{(x_{q_2} - x_{q_1})^2 + (y_{q_2} - y_{q_1})^2}}$$

Because urban canyon GPS multipath reflections intermittently inject meter-scale outliers, get_lane_distance.m:16 sorts the collected distance array in ascending order, discards both the bottom 25% and top 25% (Interquartile 25% Trimmed Mean), and averages only the central 50% of samples.

Conservative 10% Velocity-Margin Inverter Pre-Heating ETA Prediction (get_predicted_time.m)

To prevent late inverter activation as the bus approaches a charging zone, line 4 (% reduce 10 %, get_predicted_time.m:4) and line 26 (get_predicted_time.m:26) of get_predicted_time.m apply a $0.9\times$ conservative safety factor to the segment-averaged velocity $\bar{v}_k$ when estimating the remaining time-to-arrival $\widehat{\text{ETA}}$:

$$\widehat{d}_{\text{step}, k} = 0.9 \cdot \Delta d_k, \qquad \widehat{\text{ETA}}_k = \frac{d_{\text{rem}, k}}{\bar{v}_k}, \qquad \text{Inverter State} = \begin{cases} \text{ON (Active)} & \text{if } d_{\text{rem}} \le 0 \\ \text{PRE-HEAT} & \text{if } 0 \lt \widehat{\text{ETA}}_k \le \tau_{\text{warm}} \\ \text{STANDBY} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
