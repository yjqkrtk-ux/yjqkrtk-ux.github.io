5.1 Why Execute the ISP in Latent Space? — Emergent Order in High-Dimensional Chaos

Modern 16-channel foundation VAEs (FLUX.1, Stable Diffusion 3.5) compress each \(8\times 8\) RGB patch (\(8\times 8\times 3 = 192\) floats) into a single 16-dimensional latent vector \(\mathbf{z}(y,x) \in \mathbb{R}^{16}\). Previously, changing 3A/ISP parameters \(\Theta_{\mathrm{ISP}} = (\mathbf{b}, g, \mathbf{w}_{\mathrm{wb}}, M_{\mathrm{CCM}}, \gamma)\) required either a costly Decode → Pixel ISP → Re-Encode round-trip or retraining a black-box neural network.

Theoretical Foundation: "The Latent Color Subspace: Emergent Order in High-Dimensional Chaos"

Although \(\mathbb{R}^{16}\) appears entangled, it self-organizes into two nearly orthogonal subspaces (implementation: jet_basis.py:150):

  • 1. 3-D Chromatic DC Base Manifold (\(\mathbb{R}^3\)): Captures the \(8\times 8\) patch mean RGB color \(\bar{\mathbf{c}} = (\bar{R}, \bar{G}, \bar{B})^\top \in [0,1]^3\).
  • 2. 16-D Structural AC Fiber (\(\mathbb{R}^{16}\)): Captures intra-patch edges, textures, and local contrast (\(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mathrm{AC}} \in \mathbb{R}^3\)) via the residual vector \(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{AC}}\).
$$\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0(y,x) = \psi_{\mathrm{DC}}\!\left(\mathbf{z}_0(y,x)\right) = W_{\mathrm{dec}}\,\mathbf{z}_0(y,x) + \mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{dec}} \in \mathbb{R}^3$$ $$\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,x) = \mathbf{z}_0(y,x) - \phi_{\mathrm{DC}}\!\left(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0(y,x)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{16}, \qquad \phi_{\mathrm{DC}}(\bar{\mathbf{c}}) = W_{\mathrm{enc}}\,\bar{\mathbf{c}} + \mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{enc}}$$

5.2 Core Mathematical Principles: 1:4:1 Quadrature · 3×3 Secant Jacobian · 144-D Kronecker Product

Principle 1 · Why 1:4:1 Weighting? — Correcting Jensen's Inequality via Simpson / Gauss-Legendre Quadrature

Because gamma tone mapping \(f_\gamma(c) = c^{1/\gamma}\) is concave (\(f''(c) \lt 0\)), evaluating \(f_{\mathrm{ISP}}\) only at the patch mean \(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0\) suffers from Jensen's Inequality bias (\(\mathbb{E}[f_{\mathrm{ISP}}(\mathbf{x})] \lt f_{\mathrm{ISP}}(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0)\)). Evaluating three symmetric quadrature nodes \(\mathbf{c}_- = \bar{\mathbf{c}}_0 - \boldsymbol{\delta}\), \(\mathbf{c}_0 = \bar{\mathbf{c}}_0\), and \(\mathbf{c}_+ = \bar{\mathbf{c}}_0 + \boldsymbol{\delta}\) with \(\boldsymbol{\delta} = \sqrt{1.5}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mathrm{AC}}\) and weights \(\frac{1}{6} : \frac{4}{6} : \frac{1}{6}\) (\(1:4:1\)) matches the second-order Taylor expansion \(\frac{1}{2}f''(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0)\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mathrm{AC}}^2\) exactly:

$$\bar{\mathbf{c}}_4 = \frac{1}{6}\,f_{\mathrm{ISP}}(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0 - \sqrt{1.5}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mathrm{AC}}) + \frac{4}{6}\,f_{\mathrm{ISP}}(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0) + \frac{1}{6}\,f_{\mathrm{ISP}}(\bar{\mathbf{c}}_0 + \sqrt{1.5}\,\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\mathrm{AC}})$$

Principle 2 · 3×3 Secant Jacobian \(J_{3\times 3}\)

By the chain rule, intra-patch high-frequency variations transform under the composite \(3\times 3\) Jacobian matrix:

$$J_{3\times 3}(y,x) = J_4(\mathbf{c}_{3,\pm})\,M_{\mathrm{CCM}}\,\mathrm{diag}(w_R, w_G, w_B)\,(g\,I_3) \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}$$

Principle 3 · 144-D Kronecker Outer Product (\(\otimes\))

Because all 16 VAE channels entangle RGB colors and spatial frequencies, we take the Kronecker outer product between \(\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{AC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{16}\) and \(\mathrm{vec}(J_{3\times 3}) \in \mathbb{R}^9\):

$$\boldsymbol{\xi}(y,x) = \mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{AC}}(y,x) \otimes \mathrm{vec}\!\left(J_{3\times 3}(y,x)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{144}$$
