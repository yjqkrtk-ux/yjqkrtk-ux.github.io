CHAPTER 04 · UNIVERSAL CFA (universal_cfa/ · SIMULATION TRACK)

Universal CFA — Phase-Aware In-Sensor Compression & Frozen Host Translator

To overcome the photon-collection limits of sub-micron pixels, modern 50MP–200MP mobile image sensors replace the classic \(2\times 2\) Bayer pattern with Tetracell (\(4\times 4\) period) and Hexadecacell (\(8\times 8\) period) Color Filter Arrays (CFAs) that group identical color filters into \(2\times 2\) or \(4\times 4\) clusters. The Universal CFA (SE-Universal-CFA) research track bypasses costly remosaicing and full RAW transmission: a sensor-specific phase-aware SE-ResNet compressor (\(\mathcal{E}_A^{\mathrm{CFA}}\)) maps the 1-channel CFA mosaic directly into a single 16-channel bus tensor \(r \in \mathbb{R}^{16\times 64\times 64}\) (64 KB INT8) shared across a sensor-invariant unified host translator (\(\mathcal{T}_B: 16\to 16\)) and 16-channel FLUX.1 / TAEF1 decoder (\(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rec}}\)).

4.1 Stacked Sensor Architecture & Clustered CFAs (Bayer · Tetracell · Hexadecacell)

When pixel pitch shrinks to \(0.56\ \mu\text{m}\text{–}0.7\ \mu\text{m}\), the number of photons captured by each individual photodiode drops sharply, causing shot noise to explode in low light. To address this, modern high-megapixel sensors place identical-color filters over adjacent \(2\times 2\) pixels (Tetracell / Quad-Bayer, total period \(4\times 4\)) or \(4\times 4\) pixels (Hexadecacell / 16-Cell, total period \(8\times 8\)), and stack a logic and in-sensor NPU die directly beneath the pixel die via Cu-Cu hybrid bonding (cfa_physics.py).

The Double Burden of Conventional Pipelines: Remosaic + Demosaic

In low light, adjacent same-color pixels are summed (binned) to behave like a standard Bayer sensor; in daylight, however, unlocking full spatial resolution requires remosaicing—rearranging the clustered mosaic back into a standard \(2\times 2\) Bayer grid—followed immediately by demosaicing into 3-channel RGB. In Hexadecacell sensors, where same-color clusters span 4 pixels across, high-frequency edges suffer from severe false color and zippering artifacts.

Phase Scrambling under Standard Stride-2 Convolutions

What happens if we apply a standard stride=2, kernel=4×4 convolution directly to a 1-channel CFA RAW image? In Bayer (\(2\times 2\) period), every 2-pixel shift of the kernel sees the exact same [R, Gr; Gb, B] phase arrangement. In Tetracell (\(4\times 4\)) or Hexadecacell (\(8\times 8\)), however, a stride-2 shift causes the kernel to scan completely different color combinations at different spatial positions, destroying the translation equivariance of a single convolutional filter.

4.2 Interactive CFA Lattice & PixelUnshuffle Phase Decomposition Simulator

The operator that fundamentally eliminates phase shift is PixelUnshuffle(r) (Space-to-Depth) (phase_aware_compressor.py). By decomposing the input tensor \(y_{\mathrm{CFA}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times H\times W}\) into \(r^2\) sub-phase planes matched to the CFA's fundamental period \(r \in \{2, 4, 8\}\), every resulting channel contains samples of only a single color and a single spatial phase, guaranteeing exact spatial alignment:

$$\operatorname{PixelUnshuffle}_r : \mathbb{R}^{1\times H\times W} \;\longrightarrow\; \mathbb{R}^{r^2 \times \frac{H}{r} \times \frac{W}{r}}, \qquad r \in \{2\ (\text{Bayer}),\; 4\ (\text{Tetracell}),\; 8\ (\text{Hexadecacell})\}$$

4.3 Unified 16-Channel Host Translator (\(\mathcal{T}_B: 16\to 16\)) & Sensor-Specific Phase Compressors (\(\mathcal{E}_A^{\mathrm{CFA}}\))

Retraining and recompiling a large host-SoC translator (\(\mathcal{T}_B\)) and downstream decoders/diffusion models every time a smartphone or edge product line swaps camera sensor modules (Bayer, Tetracell, Hexadecacell) incurs prohibitive engineering cost. SE-Universal-CFA keeps the host's 16-channel translator (\(\mathcal{T}_B: 16\to 16\)) and decoder (\(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rec}}\)) 100% sensor-invariant (shared), adapting only the lightweight in-sensor phase-aware compressor \(\mathcal{E}_A^{\mathrm{CFA}}\) to the sensor's period \(r\):

$$r = \mathcal{E}_A^{\mathrm{CFA}}\!\big(y_{\mathrm{CFA}}\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{16\times 64\times 64}, \qquad \hat{z} = r + \mathcal{T}_B(r) \in \mathbb{R}^{16\times 64\times 64}$$
Whereas LSP Stage A in Chapter 02 takes 3-channel RGB (\(512\times 512\times 3\)) from the on-chip ISP, Universal CFA's \(\mathcal{E}_A^{\mathrm{CFA}}\) ingests the 1-channel CFA mosaic (\(512\times 512\times 1\)) prior to any on-chip demosaicing or remosaicing, projecting directly into the same 16-channel FLUX.1 / TAEF1 latent space used across HDRNet and Comp-LSP.

4.4 The No-Raw-Retention Constraint & One-Pass Latent Reconstruction

Many academic diffusion-based inverse-problem solvers (such as DPS and DDRM) reference the observation operator \(A\) (CFA mask) and raw measurement \(y_{\mathrm{CFA}}\) at every denoising step \(t = T, \dots, 1\) to compute a data-consistency gradient \(\nabla_{x_t}\lVert y_{\mathrm{CFA}} - A\hat{x}_0(x_t)\rVert_2^2\).

Why Does the LSP Bus Exclude Iterative RAW Guidance?

The foundational premise of our system is No-Raw-Retention: never transmitting the raw mosaic \(y_{\mathrm{CFA}}\) to the host at all, sending only the 64 KB tensor \(r\). Because the host never possesses \(y_{\mathrm{CFA}}\) at reconstruction time, it conditions solely on \(\hat{z} = \mathcal{T}_B(r)\) to synthesize the final RGB image via a single-pass (One-Pass) latent decoder (high_fidelity_decoder.py) or latent-conditioned diffusion backbone.

Simulation Scope & Fidelity Caveats
  • Synthetic CFA Inputs: Inputs in universal_cfa/ experiments are simulated mosaics sampled from RGB images via cfa_physics.py rather than physical RAW captures, and do not yet include on-chip IMX500 .rpk hardware measurements.
  • Referenced Bus Latency: The bus_latency_ms values (2.9 ms / 52.91 ms) recorded in benchmark_summary.json reference the physical Ch.02 LSP hardware measurements with matching payload sizes (64 KB vs 3,072 KB).
  • Latent Cosine "—" Entries: Because pixel-domain algorithmic remosaicing baselines never produce a latent tensor \(\hat{z}\), the benchmark script (run_universal_benchmark.py) logs a placeholder 0.0, rendered as — in the table.

Key Takeaways · Chapter 04

  1. Phase Scrambling in Clustered CFAs: Applying standard strided convolutions directly to Tetracell (\(4\times 4\)) and Hexadecacell (\(8\times 8\)) mosaics scans different color clusters at different spatial shifts, breaking phase alignment.
  2. PixelUnshuffle(\(r\)) + SE-ResBlock Phase Decomposition: Unshuffling the input mosaic by its fundamental period \(r \in \{2, 4, 8\}\) into \(r^2\) homogeneous phase planes followed by SE-ResBlock layers maps every CFA family into a unified 16-channel bus tensor \(r \in \mathbb{R}^{16\times 64\times 64}\) (64 KB INT8).
  3. 100% Sensor-Invariant 16-Channel Host Pipeline: Even when the camera sensor module changes, the host's 16-channel translator (\(\mathcal{T}_B: 16\to 16\)) and TAEF1 + SE-PixelShuffle decoder (\(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{rec}}\)) share 100% identical frozen weights.
  4. FLUX.1 / TAEF1 16-Channel Latent Alignment & High-Fidelity Reconstruction: Achieves 35.91 dB on Bayer (\(1\times 1\)), 34.56 dB on Tetracell (\(2\times 2\)), and 30.27 dB on Hexadecacell (\(4\times 4\)) on BJDD_CVPR21 (and 33.71 / 33.21 / 31.29 dB on Kodak24), outperforming KLAP Unified (34.06 / 29.74 dB) on both Tetracell and Hexadecacell.
